So what makes AI choose one business over another?

There isn't one known universal ranking formula. Different systems use different retrieval methods, sources and models.

But there is still something useful we can investigate.

Being good and looking recommendable are different jobs.

Your business may genuinely possess years of experience, strong reviews, specialist knowledge, good people, and an excellent reputation.

But those advantages can be buried, poorly explained, inconsistent, difficult to extract, poorly corroborated, or absent from the questions customers actually ask.

The authority can exist in the real world while remaining difficult for a machine to interpret.

How systems interpret businesses

Think of it through four concepts:

FIND

Can useful information about your business be discovered and accessed?

UNDERSTAND

Can the system clearly determine who you are, what you do, where you operate, who you serve, and what you specialise in?

VERIFY

Can important claims be supported or corroborated through your own content, credible third-party information, reviews, credentials, and consistent business facts?

COMPARE

When a customer asks a specific buying question, is enough relevant information available to understand how your business fits against alternatives?

This is why your analysis is not simply asking if your business exists online.

It is asking a much more useful set of questions.

  • When customers ask about your priority service, which businesses appear?
  • Does your business appear consistently?
  • Which competitors appear instead?
  • Does AI correctly understand what you do and where you are strongest?
  • What sources appear around the businesses being recommended?
  • Are important facts inconsistent?
  • Are valuable claims supported?
  • Do different AI systems tell the same story?
