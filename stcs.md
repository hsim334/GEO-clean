# GetCitedGEO — Selling Playbook

## Core Sales Philosophy

This document contains every selling principle, tactic, and rule that governs how GetCitedGEO communicates, sells, and closes. Every piece of copy, every ad, every reel script, every sales call, every follow-up message must follow these rules. No exceptions.

---

## THE GOLDEN RULES

### Sell Outcomes, Not the Product
Nobody cares about schema markup, llms.txt, or crawl optimisation. They care about being recommended by ChatGPT when someone searches for their service. Always frame the work in terms of what it means for their business — more visibility, more clients finding them, competitors not beating them in a channel they didn't know existed.

### If It Doesn't Make Money or Save Time, It Won't Sell
Every pitch must connect to revenue or efficiency. "AI visibility" is abstract. "Clients finding you through ChatGPT instead of your competitor" is money. Always close the loop to their bottom line.

### Remove Friction, Don't Add Features
The offer is simple: free audit, one call, clear price, we fix everything. Don't add complexity. Don't offer 5 tiers. Don't overexplain the process. The simpler the path from "interested" to "paying," the more people walk it.

### Short Messages Beat Long Explanations
Every ad, DM, email, and follow-up should be as short as possible while still being specific. If it can be said in 10 words, don't use 30. Respect their time and they'll give you more of it.

### Personal Beats Polished
A message that feels like it was written by a real person beats a corporate-sounding pitch every time. Sound like a sharp human, not a brochure.

### Don't Sound Like Marketing
The moment it reads like an ad, they scroll past. Write like you're telling a mate something interesting you found. "I ran an audit on 10 accounting firms in Melbourne and the results were wild" beats "Unlock your AI potential with our comprehensive audit solution."

### Make It Feel Like a No-Brainer
The free audit removes all risk. They give you a URL, you give them a full report. If the report is valuable, they trust you. If they trust you, they buy. The audit does the selling.

## COMMUNICATION RULES

### Get to the Point Immediately
First sentence = the hook. No preamble. No "hope you're well." No "I wanted to reach out because." Start with the thing that makes them stop and pay attention.

### Never Sound Like You're Selling
If a sentence sounds like it belongs in a sales email, delete it. Talk like a normal person who found something interesting and wants to share it.

### Keep Messages Short Enough to Get a Reply
If a message takes more than 15 seconds to read, it's too long. DMs should be 2-3 sentences. Emails should be 4-6 sentences. Ads should be readable in under 5 seconds.

### Make It Feel Like a Quick Conversation, Not a Pitch
"Hey — I ran a quick audit on your site. Thought you'd want to see the results. Free, no strings. Want me to send it over?" That's a conversation. "I'd like to introduce our GEO optimisation services which help businesses improve their AI visibility across multiple platforms" — that's a pitch. Never do the second one.

### Ask Questions That Force a Response
"Do you know what happens when someone asks ChatGPT for a [their service] in [their city]?" They can't not wonder. Questions that expose a gap they haven't considered are more powerful than any statement.

### Don't Overexplain
Tell them what. Don't tell them how. "Your site is invisible to AI — we fix that" is better than a paragraph explaining what GEO is, how AI crawlers work, and what schema markup does. Save the explanation for the call.

### Create Curiosity
"I found something interesting about your website" beats "Your website has 7 GEO issues that are reducing your AI visibility score." The first one gets opened. The second one gets skimmed and forgotten.

### Make It About Their Situation, Not Your Offer
"Your competitor shows up when someone asks ChatGPT for [their service] in Melbourne. You don't." — that's about them. "We offer comprehensive GEO optimisation services" — that's about you. Always the first, never the second.

### Use Specific Examples Instead of General Claims
"A law firm with 246 Google reviews and 20 years experience scored 19/100 on AI visibility" — specific, believable, interesting. "Most businesses have poor AI visibility" — generic, forgettable, ignorable.

### Let Them Come to the Conclusion Themselves
Show them the data. Show them the gap. Show them the competitor comparison. Then shut up. Don't tell them they need to buy. Let the evidence lead them there. People trust their own conclusions 10x more than yours.

## SALES PSYCHOLOGY

### Frame as "This Might Help" Not "Buy This"
"I ran an audit on your site — thought the results might be useful to you" removes all sales pressure. They're receiving value, not being sold to. This is the frame for every initial interaction.

### Act Like You Don't Need the Sale
Desperation repels buyers. Confidence attracts them. "The report's yours either way — if you want us to handle the fixes, just let me know" communicates that you're busy, you have other clients, and this isn't make-or-break for you. Even if it is.

### Don't Chase — If They Ghost, Re-enter Casually
"Hey — circling back on that audit. No rush at all, just wanted to make sure you got it. Happy to jump on a quick call if any of it was confusing." Light. Easy. No pressure. One follow-up. If they don't respond to that, move on.

### Let Them Sell Themselves
On the call, after showing the competitor comparison: "So based on what you're seeing here, what do you think makes sense?" Let them say "I need to fix this." That's 100x more powerful than you saying "you should fix this."

### Control the Frame of the Conversation
You're the expert. You ran the audit. You have the data. You control what gets discussed and in what order. Start with the score. Then the gaps. Then the competitors. Then the fix. Then the price. That's the frame. Don't let them derail it into "what's your experience" or "how does this compare to SEO" until after they've seen the data.

### Volume Beats Perfection
A slightly imperfect ad that runs today beats a perfect ad that runs next week. A decent follow-up sent on time beats a brilliant one sent too late. Speed of execution wins. Always.

### Use Pattern Interrupts to Get Attention
The receipt-style ad. The ChatGPT mockup. The big red "23/100" score. Anything that breaks the pattern of what people expect to see in their feed. Pattern interrupts earn you 2 seconds of attention. That's enough to deliver the hook.

### Always Have a Reason for Wanting Their Attention
"I audited businesses in your industry" — that's a reason. "I wanted to reach out" — that's not a reason. Every message needs to justify why they should care in the first 5 words.

## PRICING & VALUE PSYCHOLOGY

### Sell Results, Never Effort
The price is for the outcome (AI visibility, competitive advantage, being cited by ChatGPT), not the work (writing schema markup, creating llms.txt). Never list hours, never show time estimates, never make them think about how long things take.

### People Buy Status, Not Just Results
"Your competitors are being recommended by AI and you're not" hits harder than "you could get more traffic from AI." It's not just about results — it's about not being left behind. Ego is a stronger driver than logic.

### Make Them Look Better to Others
For the business owner: "Imagine a potential client asks ChatGPT for a [their service] and your firm comes up first." That's status. That's something they'd tell their partners about. Frame the result as something that makes them look smart for investing in.

### Attach Your Offer to Something They Already Value
They already value Google reviews, SEO, their website, their reputation. GEO is the next layer on top of what they already care about. "You've invested in a great website and strong reviews — GEO makes sure AI can actually see all of that."

### Perceived Effort Should Be Low, Perceived Value Should Be High
"We handle everything — you don't need to touch a thing" = low effort for them. "Your business becomes visible to 100 million AI users" = high value. The wider that gap, the easier the sale.

### People Want Leverage, Not More Work
Position the service as leverage: "You've already done the hard part — built the business, earned the reviews, created the content. We just make sure AI can see it."

### Make Them Feel Early, Not Late
"GEO is where SEO was in 2005. The businesses that move now will own AI search for the next decade." Early adopter energy. Insider advantage. If they feel late, they feel stupid. If they feel early, they feel smart.

### People Copy What Others Are Already Doing
"We're working with 6 other professional services firms in Melbourne on this right now." Social proof. If their peers are doing it, it must be worth doing. Even if you only have 1-2 clients, frame it as a growing trend in their industry.

### The First Win Must Feel Immediate
"Within the first week, your llms.txt file will be live and AI crawlers will be able to understand your site properly for the first time." Give them something that happens fast, even if the full results take 60-90 days.

## URGENCY & TIMING

### If They Can Delay It, They Won't Buy It
Create a reason to act now without being salesy. "AI search is growing 500% year over year. Every month you wait, your competitors are building more AI authority that you'll have to overcome." Time-based framing, not artificial scarcity.

### Tie Your Offer to Money Already Being Lost
"You're already losing potential clients to competitors who show up in AI search and you don't. This isn't about spending new money — it's about recovering revenue you're already missing."

### Make the Problem Feel Expensive
"If even one client a month is choosing a competitor because ChatGPT recommended them instead of you, that's $X in lost revenue per year." Make them do the maths in their head. A $2,000 accounting client lost every month = $24,000/year. The $1,500 setup fee suddenly feels like nothing.

### Products Tied to Events Convert Better Than Always-On Offers
"AI search just crossed 100 million weekly users" is an event. "Google just announced AI Overviews in Australia" is an event. Tie your outreach to real moments of change. People act when something shifts, not when things are stable.

---

## TRUST & CLARITY

### Confusion Kills Trust Instantly
If they don't understand what you do in 5 seconds, you've lost them. "We make your business visible to ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity." That's it. That's the explanation. Everything else is detail for the call.

### Every Step Should Feel Obvious
Fill out the form → book a call → see your report → decide. No hidden steps, no surprise requirements, no "we'll get back to you within 3-5 business days." The path is clear and short.

### Make It Reversible, Even If It Isn't
"The report is yours to keep regardless." "No lock-in contracts." "If you're not happy with the setup, we'll revise it." Reduce perceived risk before increasing value.

### People Want to Feel in Control of the Decision
Never pressure. Never create false urgency. Give them the information, give them the report, give them the price, and let them decide. "Take your time — happy to answer any questions" communicates confidence and respect.

### Trust Is Built Faster with Specificity Than Authority
"Your site is missing LegalService schema, has no llms.txt file, and your 246 Google reviews aren't surfaced via AggregateRating markup" builds more trust than "we're Australia's leading GEO agency." Specificity proves expertise. Claims don't.

### You Don't Need Brand Early, You Need Proof
One real audit with real numbers is worth more than a perfect logo, a polished website, and a mission statement. Lead with the audit. The brand will follow the results.

## OFFER DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### If You Can't Explain It in One Sentence, It's Too Complex
"We audit your website's AI visibility and fix everything that's stopping ChatGPT from recommending you." That's the offer. One sentence.

### The Product Should Feel Obvious Once Seen
When they see the audit showing their score at 23/100 and their competitor at 67/100, the product is obvious. They need to be at 67+. You do that. No further explanation needed.

### If They Need Education, the Funnel Is Broken
The audit IS the education. The competitor comparison IS the education. If you have to spend 10 minutes explaining what GEO is before they understand why they need it, the report didn't do its job. Fix the report, not the sales pitch.

### Make the Product Fit Into What They Already Do
They already have a website. They already care about being found online. They already know ChatGPT exists. GEO slots into their existing mental model — it's not a new category they have to learn, it's the next step in something they already understand.

### You Only Need One Strong Use Case to Win Early
"When someone asks ChatGPT for a [their service] in [their city], you show up." That's the one use case. Don't dilute it with "also helps with Perplexity and Gemini and Google AI Overviews and..." Lead with ChatGPT because everyone knows it. Expand later.

### Distribution Matters More Than Product
A mediocre audit that gets in front of 100 business owners will outperform a perfect audit that sits on your hard drive. Focus on getting the audit in front of people — that's what ads, reels, and content do.

### If You Have to Convince Them It's Valuable, It Isn't Yet
If the audit doesn't make them go "oh shit, I need to fix this," the audit needs work, not your sales pitch. The report should sell itself. If it doesn't, improve the report.

### Needs Beat Nice-to-Haves
"Your competitors are being recommended and you're not" = need. "You could get additional traffic from AI" = nice-to-have. Always frame as a need, never as a bonus.

### You Need a Clear Before and After State
Before: invisible to AI, competitors getting recommended, score 23/100. After: cited by ChatGPT, showing up in AI answers, score 71/100. The clearer and more specific the transformation, the easier it sells.

### You Can Manufacture Pain by Quantifying Loss
They don't feel the pain of being invisible to AI because they've never measured it. The audit creates the measurement. The competitor comparison creates the context. Now they feel it. The audit doesn't discover pain — it manufactures it by making the invisible visible.

## FOLLOW-UP RULES

### Give Before You Ask
The free audit is the give. The call is the ask. Never reverse this order. Never ask for a call before they've seen value. Never ask for money before they've seen the report.

### Follow Up or Lose the Lead
One follow-up 2 days after the call if they didn't close. One more 5 days later. After that, one final "no hard feelings" message at day 14. That's 3 follow-ups total. Casual, light, zero pressure.

### Most Leads Convert on the Follow-Up, Not the First Touch
The first touch is awareness. The follow-up is where they've had time to think, check if they show up in ChatGPT themselves (they don't), and realise you were right. Be there when that happens.

### Silence Is Feedback, Not Randomness
If they don't respond, something is unclear or unconvincing. Don't send louder messages. Send clearer ones. Or a different angle. "Totally understand if the timing isn't right — just wanted to make sure the report made sense. Any questions at all?"

## CONTENT RULES

### The Story Around the Offer Matters as Much as the Offer
"I started GetCitedGEO because I audited my own family's law firm and discovered they were completely invisible to AI despite 20 years in business and thousands of cases" — that's a story that makes the offer human and believable.

### If It's Boring, It Won't Spread
Every piece of content needs a hook that creates a reaction. Surprise, curiosity, outrage, or recognition. "I audited 10 firms and they all scored under 30" — that's surprising. "AI visibility is important for your business" — that's boring. Boring dies in the feed.

### Make It Feel Like an Insider Advantage
"Most businesses don't know this exists yet" creates the feeling of early access. They're getting information their competitors don't have. That's shareable. That's memorable.

### Clarity Is More Persuasive Than Hype
"Your site scored 23/100 on AI visibility" persuades more than "unlock the incredible power of AI-driven discovery!" Clarity builds trust. Hype triggers scepticism.

### You Don't Need to Be Unique, Just Clearly Better in One Way
You don't need to invent a new category. You need to be the most specific, most data-driven, most no-bullshit GEO service in Australia. That's the positioning. Not unique — just clearly sharper than everyone else.

### Positioning Over Persuasion
If you're positioned correctly, you don't need to persuade. "The first GEO-specialist agency in Australia" is positioning. "Please consider our services for your AI optimisation needs" is persuasion. Positioning wins.

---

## THE CARDINAL SINS — NEVER DO THESE

1. Never include time estimates in client-facing materials
2. Never list features without connecting them to outcomes
3. Never use jargon without a one-sentence plain English explanation
4. Never send a long message when a short one will do
5. Never pitch before providing value
6. Never guarantee specific AI rankings or citation counts
7. Never sound desperate, needy, or overly eager
8. Never use corporate language ("synergise," "leverage," "holistic solution")
9. Never make it about you when it should be about them
10. Never let a lead go more than 48 hours without a response
